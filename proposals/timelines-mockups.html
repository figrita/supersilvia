Timeline Tabs

A timeline is a third kind of tab beside video and audio workspaces. It holds no nodes. It references nodes from any workspace: an Output to composite, any other node to automate. Drawn to be argued with, not to be built from.

1. What has what

silvia's audio branch already gives a workspace a type, and the type picks the compiler and which nodes the menu offers. A timeline is a tab whose type owns no nodes and no compiler. It owns references into the one graph, a transport, a viewport, and a compositor. Two rules are the spine. Yours: a lane owns exactly the address it drives, and nothing else is timeline-controlled. And: an Output on a timeline is a clip. Nothing nests. Nothing is used twice, except that the same node may be referenced by more than one timeline, and that is the one new fork.

What this settles that the meditation had open, and what it leaves:

  • The layer is the Output. No layer node, and the "which Output is the layer" question is gone: every Output on the timeline is one.
  • Nothing nests. One graph, silvia's shape. Two clips with one look are two Outputs. The instancing question never arises. The price is that a patch is not a reusable unit; a node that is a whole patch could be added later and would fit this as an ordinary node with an Output.
  • Extent is render on and off. An Output outside its clip does not render. Cost scales with what is on stage now, not with the whole show.
  • A lane at a gap is the port unplugged. The lane is a fourth writer, present or absent, not a replacement. Outside a media clip the input shows its picker or the hue wheel.
  • Time warps only where a lane sits. A media clip's speed on its lane, or an automation lane on a node's time port. No subtree retime. That is push, settled by the product.
  • A timeline is saved with the session, referencing nodes by id, the way a workspace's visibility set does. A node deleted from its workspace leaves a dead reference the timeline reports rather than hides.
  • The cross-workspace tags carry over unchanged. A node on a timeline shows a pill in its workspace saying which timeline and which lane; the lane header shows the node and its workspace. Click either to go there.
  • The transform is on the clip. Two timelines can place one Output differently. The cost is a second owner of addresses: the graph owns node controls, a timeline owns clip controls, and lanes, MIDI and gizmos write both through one address space. silvia's mixer made the same call, channel assignment being mixer state.

2. The timeline tab

The overview. The viewport is the composite; the timeline has two regions the way Final Cut stacks different kinds of lane vertically. Above the divider, Output clips, and their vertical order is z. Below it, node track groups, one per node that has any lane, each holding parameter lanes. Double-clicking a clip or a group opens the graph at that node. Any node from any workspace can be dragged onto it: an Output lands in the compositing region, anything else opens a track group. Dragging a file onto the viewport makes a video node and an Output clip in the active workspace; dragging it onto a color input's lane makes a media track there, owned by that node; a track dropped in the free region can be bound to any number of inputs.

3. What a jump does

Your rule, drawn. A cue jumps the transport from 1:12 back to 0:20. Lane-driven values are functions of time, so they take the value the lane holds at 0:20. Everything else is state and keeps going: the feedback trail decays from where it was, the slew slides from where it was, the camera is the camera. Render mode is the one exception, and it is a mode, not a different show.

4. Today's practice, mapped

Right now people open many workspaces and live-mix them on the A/B main mixer. Nothing here removes that. It becomes a timeline tab with two Output clips on stage, and the crossfader becomes an address a lane or a MIDI fader can drive. The workspaces stay exactly where they are.
